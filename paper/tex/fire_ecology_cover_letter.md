The manuscript belongs to two of the journal's listed topics, wildfire planning
and risk management, and fire science and modeling. Fire susceptibility maps support prevention
planning in Mediterranean landscapes, and agencies without a long local fire record, for example after
a first large fire, often have to use a map trained elsewhere. We tested whether such maps transfer,
using five large Mediterranean fire seasons of 2021 and 2022: Manavgat and Muğla (Türkiye), Bejís
(Spain), North Evia (Greece) and Montiferru (Italy). Fire is the process studied throughout: the
response is the burned footprint of each season, and the predictors describe the land surface and its
vegetation before the fire.

Pre-fire satellite land surface temperature and dryness
predictors improved susceptibility within every region (+0.045 to +0.148 ROC-AUC at 5 km blocking), but
the gain carried to other regions was close to zero (+0.007 [−0.020, +0.038]), and none of twenty
similarity measures told in advance which transfers would work. Hotter surfaces burned less in four of
five regions, which is consistent with land surface temperature tracking canopy cover and terrain
more closely than fuel dryness. The study also shows that the area on which a model is scored changes its
apparent skill: the same models lost 0.160 ROC-AUC when scored next to the fire instead of
region-wide. For fire managers, this means that a borrowed susceptibility model should be tested
against burned areas in the target region before it guides prevention planning, and that every
reported score should name the area it refers to. Each region contributes one fire season, and the manuscript
states throughout that the results describe large single-season events.
