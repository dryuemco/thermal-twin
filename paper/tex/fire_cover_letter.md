Wildfire susceptibility models built from satellite predictors are usually validated in the region
where they were trained, and their skill in other regions is rarely measured. We added six pre-fire
land surface temperature and dryness predictors from Landsat and MODIS to a static baseline, trained
random forests in five Mediterranean regions with one large fire season each (Manavgat and Muğla,
Türkiye; Bejís, Spain; North Evia, Greece; Montiferru, Italy), and measured two things that are
usually assumed. First, the evaluation area changes the score: the same model lost 0.160 ROC-AUC
when it was scored on the burn scar and a 2 km collar instead of the whole study area. Second, local
skill transferred at most weakly: the thermal predictors added +0.045 to +0.148 within regions but
only +0.007 across twenty transfer directions, and none of twenty similarity measures predicted
which transfers would work.

The manuscript fits the scope of *Fire* because it concerns the science and technology of fire
susceptibility mapping: how pre-fire satellite observations of the land surface relate to where fires
burn, and how such models should be evaluated before fire managers use a model built in another
region. All satellite inputs are public, and the analysis code, the modelling datasets and the frozen
outputs behind every reported number are archived at Zenodo, so every result can be re-run.
