Fire agencies in regions without a long burned-area record often have to use susceptibility models
built elsewhere, yet such models are almost always validated only in the region where they were
trained. We tested this directly for five large Mediterranean fire seasons of 2021 and 2022 (Manavgat
and Muğla, Türkiye; Bejís, Spain; North Evia, Greece; Montiferru, Italy). Pre-fire satellite land
surface temperature and dryness predictors improved random-forest susceptibility within every region
(+0.045 to +0.148 ROC-AUC), but the gain carried to other regions was close to zero (+0.007), and no
similarity measure told in advance which transfers would work. The paper also shows that the area on
which a model is scored changes its apparent skill substantially, and that pre-fire land surface
temperature behaved as a descriptor of the land surface rather than as a dryness signal: hotter
surfaces burned less in four of the five regions.

The manuscript fits the scope of *Fire* because it concerns how fire susceptibility maps, which serve
prevention planning, should be evaluated before they are used in a new region. It builds on
susceptibility and fuel-moisture work published in *Fire* (Trucchia et al., 2022; Marino et al., 2024)
and complements weather-driven danger rating such as the Fire Weather Index used by EFFIS, by isolating
the pre-fire surface component. Each region contributes one fire season, and we state throughout that
the results describe large single-season events. All satellite inputs are public, and the analysis
code, the modelling datasets and the frozen outputs behind every reported number are archived at
Zenodo, so every result can be re-run.
