# Abstract

**Background.** Wildfire susceptibility models are evaluated where they were trained, yet agencies with few fire records may borrow one.

**Aims.** To measure how the evaluation area changes skill and whether pre-fire thermal predictors transfer.

**Methods.** Random forests were trained in five Mediterranean regions, each with one fire season, with and without land surface temperature (LST) and dryness predictors. Skill was the spatially blocked area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), within and between regions.

**Key results.** In an exploratory test, the same models lost 0.160 AUC (95 % interval 0.090 to 0.230) when scored on the burn scar and a 2 km collar instead of region-wide. Thermal predictors added +0.045 to +0.148 within regions but +0.007 (−0.020 to +0.038) in transfer. None of twenty similarity measures was shown to predict transfer. Hotter surfaces burned less in four of five regions, two with interval support.

**Conclusions.** Skill in one region does not show that a model works in another, and the signs are consistent with LST describing land surface more than fuel dryness.

**Implications.** A borrowed model needs a local test before use, which five years of earlier burns did not provide, and scores should name their evaluation area.

# Online summary

Maps of where wildfires are likely to burn guide prevention work, and a region with few past fires may have to borrow a mapping model built elsewhere. We trained models on five large Mediterranean fires and found that models that worked well at home ranked burned land little better than chance in another region. A model from elsewhere therefore needs a local test before it guides planning, and a short record of earlier fires was not enough for one.
