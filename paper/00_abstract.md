# Abstract

Wildfire susceptibility maps support prevention planning, yet are usually trained and evaluated in one region, and after a first large fire a model trained elsewhere may be the only one available. We tested such transfer in five Mediterranean regions, each with one large fire season in 2021 or 2022, namely Manavgat and Muğla (Türkiye), Bejís (Spain), North Evia (Greece) and Montiferru (Italy). Random forests used a terrain, land-cover and greenness baseline with and without six pre-fire land surface temperature (LST) and dryness predictors. Skill was the spatially blocked area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) against Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) burned areas. Three results bear on hazard assessment. First, the evaluation area alone changed skill. Scored on the burn scar and a 2 km collar instead of region-wide, the same models lost 0.160 ROC-AUC on average (95% interval [0.090, 0.230]). Second, thermal predictors added +0.045 to +0.148 within regions at 5 km blocking, but only +0.007 [−0.020, +0.038] across twenty directions and +0.024 [−0.004, +0.049] on comparable areas. Third, none of twenty similarity measures was shown to predict transfer, and at point estimates the most similar pair of regions failed in both directions. Hotter surfaces burned less in four of five regions (two with interval support), consistent with LST describing land surface more than fuel dryness. Region and event effects are confounded. A model trained elsewhere should be tested against local burned areas before guiding prevention planning, and every score should name its evaluation area.
