# Abstract

Wildfire susceptibility models are usually tested where they were trained; their skill elsewhere is rarely measured. Six pre-fire Landsat and MODIS land surface temperature and dryness predictors were added to a terrain, land-cover and greenness baseline. Random forests were trained and tested in five regions, each with one large fire season: Manavgat and Muğla 2021 (Türkiye), Bejís 2022 (Spain), North Evia 2021 (Greece) and Montiferru 2021 (Italy). Skill was the spatially blocked area under the ROC curve (ROC-AUC), with burned areas from MODIS MCD64A1. Scored on the burn scar and a 2 km collar instead of the whole region, the same model lost 0.160 [0.090, 0.230] ROC-AUC across five regions. With 5 km blocking, the thermal predictors added +0.045 to +0.148 within regions, but only +0.007 [−0.020, +0.038] across twenty transfer directions and +0.024 [−0.004, +0.049] on comparable areas. The static baseline transferred no better, and two label-free adaptation methods did not help. None of twenty similarity measures predicted transfer; at the point estimates, the most similar pair of regions failed in both directions. With one fire season per region, region and event effects are confounded. Transfer should be measured in the target region before a model is used there.
