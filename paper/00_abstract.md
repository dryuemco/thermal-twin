# Abstract

Wildfire susceptibility models are rarely tested outside their training region, yet agencies lacking fire records may borrow them. Six pre-fire land surface temperature (LST) and dryness predictors extended a terrain, land-cover and greenness baseline. Random forests were fitted in five Mediterranean regions, each with one large fire season (2021 or 2022): Manavgat and Muğla (Türkiye), Bejís (Spain), North Evia (Greece) and Montiferru (Italy). Skill was the spatially blocked area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) against Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) burned areas. Scored on the burn scar and a 2 km collar, not region-wide, the same models lost 0.160 [0.090, 0.230] ROC-AUC on average. At 5 km blocking, thermal predictors added +0.045 to +0.148 within regions, but only +0.007 [−0.020, +0.038] across twenty directions and +0.024 [−0.004, +0.049] on comparable areas. None of twenty similarity measures was shown to predict transfer. At point estimates, the most similar pair of regions failed in both directions, and hotter surfaces burned less in four of five regions (two with interval support), consistent with LST describing land surface rather than dryness. Region and event effects are confounded. Borrowed single-season models should be tested in the target region before use in prevention planning.
