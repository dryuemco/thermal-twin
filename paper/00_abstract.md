# Abstract

Wildfire susceptibility models are usually tested inside the region where they were trained. How well they work in other regions is rarely measured. In this study, six pre-fire land surface temperature and dryness predictors from Landsat and MODIS were added to a terrain, land-cover and greenness baseline. Random forests were trained and tested in five large Mediterranean fires: Manavgat and Muğla 2021 (Türkiye), Bejís 2022 (Spain), North Evia 2021 (Greece) and Montiferru 2021 (Italy). Burned areas were taken from the MODIS MCD64A1 product. Skill was measured as the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) with spatially blocked validation. Three results were found. First, the evaluation area changes the score. The same model was scored on the burn scar and a 2 km band around it, instead of the whole study region. This cost 0.133 ROC-AUC [0.060, 0.207] over seven scars, and 0.160 [0.090, 0.230] with the region as the unit. Second, local skill transferred at most weakly. The thermal predictors added +0.045 to +0.148 within regions, but only +0.007 [−0.020, +0.038] across twenty transfer directions and +0.024 [−0.004, +0.049] when the study areas were made comparable. The static baseline did not transfer better, and label-free domain adaptation did not help. Third, similarity between regions did not predict transfer: the most similar pair failed in both directions (0.438, 0.345). Transfer skill should therefore be measured in the target region before a model is used there.
