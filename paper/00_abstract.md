# Abstract

Wildfire susceptibility models are rarely tested outside their training region, yet agencies without long fire records may borrow them. Six pre-fire satellite land surface temperature (LST) and dryness predictors were added to a terrain, land-cover and greenness baseline. Random forests were trained and tested in five Mediterranean regions, each with one large fire season: Manavgat and Muğla 2021 (Türkiye), Bejís 2022 (Spain), North Evia 2021 (Greece) and Montiferru 2021 (Italy). Skill was the spatially blocked area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC) against Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) burned areas. Scored on the burn scar and a 2 km collar rather than region-wide, one model lost 0.160 [0.090, 0.230] ROC-AUC. With 5 km blocking, the thermal predictors added +0.045 to +0.148 within regions, but only +0.007 [−0.020, +0.038] across twenty transfer directions and +0.024 [−0.004, +0.049] on comparable areas. None of twenty similarity measures predicted transfer. At the point estimates, the most similar pair of regions failed in both directions, and hotter surfaces burned less in four of five regions, so LST described the land surface rather than dryness. Region and event effects are confounded. Borrowed models should be tested in the target region before use in prevention planning.
