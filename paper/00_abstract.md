# Abstract

Wildfire susceptibility models are usually tested in the region where they were trained, and how well they work elsewhere is rarely measured. Six pre-fire land surface temperature and dryness predictors from Landsat and MODIS were added to a terrain, land-cover and greenness baseline. Random forests were then trained and tested in five regions, each with one large fire season: Manavgat and Muğla 2021 (Türkiye), Bejís 2022 (Spain), North Evia 2021 (Greece) and Montiferru 2021 (Italy). Burned areas were taken from MODIS MCD64A1, and skill was measured as the area under the ROC curve (ROC-AUC) under spatially blocked validation. First, the evaluation area changes the score. When the same model was scored on the burn scar and a 2 km collar instead of the whole study area, ROC-AUC fell by 0.160 [0.090, 0.230] across five regions. The two areas answer different questions. Second, local skill transferred at most weakly. With 5 km blocking, the thermal predictors added +0.045 to +0.148 within regions, but only +0.007 [−0.020, +0.038] across twenty transfer directions and +0.024 [−0.004, +0.049] when the study areas were made comparable. The static baseline did not transfer better, and the two label-free adaptation methods tested did not help. Third, none of twenty similarity measures predicted transfer; at the point estimates, even the most similar pair of regions failed in both directions. With one fire season per region, region and event effects cannot be separated. Transfer skill should therefore be measured in the target region before a model is used there.
