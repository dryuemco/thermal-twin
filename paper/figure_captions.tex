  transfer ROC-AUC for the 20 ordered directions; natural-vegetation population; original study
  areas. \textbf{(a)} Raw transfer. \textbf{(b)} After region-wise z-scoring. \textbf{(c)} After
  CORAL. Rows are source regions and columns are target regions (Man.\ = Manavgat, Bej.\ = Bej\'is,

  falls further below chance (0.314 to 0.302). Hatching uses the exact value, so three cells printed

  Table~S16.}

\begin{figure}[htbp]

  \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\includegraphics[width=140mm]{figures/fig5_adaptation.pdf}}
  \caption{\textbf{Label-free adaptation moves transfer toward chance.} The 12 ordered directions
  among Manavgat, Bej\'is, Mu\u{g}la and Evia, the four regions of the pipeline's gap decomposition;
  Montiferru is not part of that decomposition. Natural-vegetation population; original study areas.
  The open circle is raw transfer ROC-AUC. The arrow ends at the better of the two adapted results
  (z-score or CORAL). This choice uses target labels, so the arrows show an optimistic case. The grey
  tick is the target's within-region reference at 1 km blocking, and the dashed line is chance. \textbf{Solid blue arrows (five) move toward the
  reference; dashed orange arrows (seven) move away from it.} Eleven of twelve directions end closer
  to chance. The exception is Manavgat$\rightarrow$Mu\u{g}la (0.438 to 0.427).
  Bej\'is$\rightarrow$Manavgat is shown as a recovery because CORAL raises it from 0.314 to 0.406;
  under z-scoring it falls to 0.302 (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-matrix}). The largest loss is
  Evia$\rightarrow$Manavgat, from 0.677 to 0.417, against a reference of 0.908 (recovered fraction
  $-1.126$). No arrow reaches its reference.}
  \label{fig:adaptation}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\includegraphics[width=140mm]{figures/fig6_loro.pdf}}
  \caption{\textbf{Pooling the other regions helps one target of five.} For each target (row), a
  model trained on the other four regions together (filled orange circle, with its 95\%
  spatial-block, $\approx$5\,km, bootstrap interval below the row) is compared with the mean of the
  four single-source models (open grey square) and the best single source (open blue diamond; this
  choice uses target labels). The grey tick is the within-region ceiling, and the dashed line is
  chance. Natural-vegetation population; thermal feature set; no adaptation. The pooled model is
  better than the mean single source only for Evia, at 0.715 [0.668, 0.757] against 0.569, and
  there it is also better than the best single source, 0.654. For Manavgat and Bej\'is it is below
  chance at the point estimate (0.426 and 0.458). Only the Manavgat interval lies fully below chance.
  All points are far below the within-region ceiling.}
  \label{fig:loro}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \makebox[\linewidth][c]{\includegraphics[width=140mm]{figures/fig7_feature_drop.pdf}}
  \caption{\textbf{Removing the reversing features costs local skill and gives no measurable
  transfer gain.} Mean within-region ROC-AUC over five regions (horizontal) against mean transfer
  ROC-AUC over 20 directions (vertical), for four feature sets: the full thermal set, without the
  LST anomaly, without elevation, and without both. Along the grey path, within-region skill falls
  (0.896 $\rightarrow$ 0.820) and mean transfer rises (0.527 $\rightarrow$ 0.541). The within-region
  cost is 0.076 AUC and is supported in every region. The change in transfer is $+$0.014 AUC, with a
  pair-$t$ interval over ten region pairs of [$-$0.028, $+$0.056], which includes zero. The vertical
  whisker shows this interval around the full-set transfer level (dotted line).}
  \label{fig:feature-drop}
\end{figure}
