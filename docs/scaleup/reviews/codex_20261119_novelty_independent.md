## Reviewer verdict

Re-centre the paper on **the cost of selecting a wildfire model on the focal scar when the intended use is ranking later burning**. That is the strongest empirical result. It is a novel application of an established evaluation idea, with direct precedents in species distribution modelling and spatial ML. The reference models and proper scores make the paper more defensible, but contribute little methodological novelty on their own.

**EMS fit is credible, with substantial desk-reject risk in the current state.** The repository supports a retrospective selection result and a frozen-score temporal replication for coordinate-only models. It does not yet establish the proposed comparison of calibrated forecasts against fire history. A clean evaluation could be publishable even if the history reference wins.

## 2. Novelty and defensibility

**P1 — Use-specific references.** Hijmans already introduced the nearest-training-presence diagnostic and cAUC. The current manuscript itself already distinguishes within-event diagnosis from a legitimate historical comparator. Preisler et al. compared wildfire probabilities with spatially and temporally smoothed historic fire frequencies. The exact 2 km benchmark and the comparison of history alone with history plus environmental predictors are useful standardisation choices. Specify a common issuance cutoff, target, domain and permitted data vintages. Use nested comparisons to measure the increment from environment. Holding out a fire geographically still permits temporal leakage if other training fires or calibration outcomes postdate issuance. An observed ignition supports a separate conditional spread task with its own issue time and verification target. [Hijmans, DOI 10.1890/11-0826.1](https://doi.org/10.1890/11-0826.1) · [Preisler et al., DOI 10.1071/WF06162](https://www.fs.usda.gov/psw/publications/preisler/psw_2008_preisler001.pdf)

**P2 — Later-burning verification.** Moran et al. benchmarked annual burn-probability maps against subsequent California wildfires using reference-relative logarithmic skill. Trucchia et al. trained seasonal susceptibility maps on Italian historical fires and evaluated against independent 2021 scars. CSEP provides the precedent for relative log likelihood and alarm-area tradeoffs. Use an out-of-sample Bernoulli log-score difference over all observable cells, including negatives, and specify its units and aggregation. A per-site log-score gain and a CSEP per-event point-process information gain are different quantities. For Molchan-style capture, define whether the horizontal axis is physical area or reference probability mass. Give the history comparator the same physical area budget for the operational comparison. A Diebold–Mariano time-series test has no natural ordering across fire sites. Use paired cluster-level loss differences and intervals, with sensitivity to shared fire seasons and large events. [Moran et al., DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-07968-6](https://pyrologix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/s41598-025-07968-6.pdf) · [Trucchia et al., DOI 10.3390/fire5010030](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-6255/5/1/30) · [CSEP comparative tests](https://docs.cseptesting.org/getting_started/theory.html)

**P3 — Selection consequence.** This is the best contribution, with clear prior art across the fields searched.

- **Ecology and SDM.** Baker et al. directly asked whether internal CV selected the best parameterisation on independent test data. It selected the independent-AUC winner in only **15–32%** of models, depending on the setting. Finn et al. compared internal AUC rankings of candidate distribution models with rankings from an independent field survey. [Baker et al., DOI 10.1111/ddi.13802](https://renewbiodiversity.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Diversity-and-Distributions-2024-Baker-Effective-strategies-for-correcting-spatial-sampling-bias-in-species.pdf) · [Finn et al., DOI 10.1111/1365-2664.14616](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.14616)
- **Spatial ML.** Milà et al. explicitly tested whether validation procedures rank RFs with and without spatial proxies correctly against known prediction performance. Random CV tended to favour spatial proxies in unsuitable settings. That is the closest methodological precedent for P3 and P4 together. [Milà et al., DOI 10.5194/gmd-17-6007-2024](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/6007/2024/)
- **Hydrology and forecast verification.** Arsenault et al. tested whether calibration and validation strategies yielded parameter sets that performed well on an untouched eight-year period. Forecast verification similarly has a long tradition of comparing selection and evaluation procedures against later outcomes using rolling or fixed origins. [Arsenault et al., author manuscript](https://espace2.etsmtl.ca/id/eprint/17397/1/Arsenault-R-2018-17397.pdf) · [Forecast evaluation under stationarity, DOI 10.1002/for.3013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/for.3013)
- **Wildfire.** Trucchia provides spatial CV plus later independent scars, and Moran provides later-burning verification and benchmarks. I found no demonstrated match in those papers for the specific pairwise regret calculation comparing pooled AUC, Hijmans cAUC and stratified AUC as focal-scar selectors. That combination and its replication support an empirical contribution. The literature search does not establish priority across wildfire research. [Trucchia et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-6255/5/1/30) · [Moran et al.](https://pyrologix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/s41598-025-07968-6.pdf)

**P4 — Known-truth simulation.** Milà is direct precedent for testing spatial model selection against known prediction performance. The use of observed fire grids and matched burn prevalence makes this an incremental stress test. Present it as evidence that metric performance depends on the transfer scenario, with the real-data P3 result carrying the main argument. [Milà et al., DOI 10.5194/gmd-17-6007-2024](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/6007/2024/)

**P5 — Covariate-adjusted AUC.** Janes–Pepe standardises discrimination against the control score distribution conditional on a covariate. This answers a different question from Hijmans’ matching and residual null correction. The cross-fitted distance covariate, target weighting, common support and extrapolation need explicit treatment. Baddeley et al. also develop spatial ROC diagnostics for model selection and baseline adjustment. A win in the Claim 13 simulation alone would be weak grounds to replace cAUC. If run, report the comparison regardless of which metric wins and keep cAUC for continuity with the registered analysis. [Janes and Pepe 2008, DOI 10.1093/aje/kwn099](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=13399eb6412e2f9b783c852d3c43540b1d330330&repid=rep1&type=pdf) · [Janes and Pepe 2009, DOI 10.1093/biomet/asp002](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3371718/) · [Baddeley et al., arXiv:2506.03414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03414)

**P6 — Factorial negative controls.** Beale et al. fitted climate envelopes to null species distributions preserving key spatial properties. Ridder et al. used shift-and-rotate environmental null predictors in SDMs, including spatial CV. Crossing the two controls is a plausible incremental design. The existing placebo-collar analysis held the original out-of-fold scores and real positives fixed and moved the negative sampling frame. Its mean placebo AUC was **0.783**, versus **0.784** for the original frame and **0.635** for the actual collar. The proposed design changes labels and refits models, so the existing result cannot stand in for it. Use repeated eligible placements, preserve burnable area and fold support, and validate joint predictor structure as well as marginal variograms. A real-versus-placebo interaction can reflect terrain or mask constraints on where scars fit. [Beale et al., DOI 10.1073/pnas.0803506105](https://sciences.ucf.edu/biology/d4lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/23/2020/08/Beale-etal-2008.pdf) · [Ridder et al., DOI 10.1111/2041-210X.14443](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.14443) · [Placebo report](/C:/Users/CORSAIR/projects/ThermalTwin/thermal-twin-main/paper/ems_analyses/geometry/REPORT.md)

**P7 — UAV patrol.** Move it to a separate paper. The exporter defaults to `lst_anomaly_mean` and filters valid, finite cells. The planner selects top-*k* cells, splits them round-robin, orders each vehicle’s targets by nearest neighbour and adds a return waypoint. Those two files do not establish nine-minute feasibility, sensed footprints or detection performance. WFDroneBench already combines risk maps, routing strategies and simulated detection in **7,746 scenarios across 49 locations**. A distinct UAV contribution would need measured or realistically simulated detection under equal feasible mission budgets. Capturing pixels in eventual burn scars evaluates a different outcome from detecting incipient fires. [WFDroneBench, arXiv:2609.11829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11829) · [Route planner](/C:/Users/CORSAIR/projects/bap/thermoswarm-cal/ros2_ws/src/thermoswarm_satellite/src/planning.cpp:112) · [Grid exporter](/C:/Users/CORSAIR/projects/bap/thermoswarm-cal/ros2_ws/src/thermoswarm_satellite/exporter/export_priority_grid.py:34)

## 3. Wording and editorial decision

The proposed sentence overclaims in three places. Fire history supplies predictors. The 31-fire diagnostic and the 18-site later-burning comparisons have different denominators. The replicated result concerns **coordinate-only** learners against a specified thermal RF, and the proposed proper-score benchmark is still unverified.

> We evaluate spatial cross-validation on 31 large Mediterranean fires and test whether it selects models that rank later burning well in the same landscapes. Among 18 eligible sites observed through 2023 and 18 eligible sites in a frozen-score replication for January 2024 to September 2026, pooled within-event AUC always preferred a coordinate-only random forest to a thermal and environmental random forest. The coordinate-only forest averaged 0.116 and 0.069 lower AUC on later burning, respectively. We use distance to training burns to diagnose within-event splits and specify a pre-issuance fire-history reference for evaluating susceptibility forecasts.

If P2 is completed, add the estimated skill against that reference and its interval. A finding that none of the tested candidates shows a practically meaningful gain can carry the paper if the interval excludes the declared meaningful gain. Avoid “first” claims for distance-based nulls, use-specific benchmarks, smoothed fire-history references, temporal wildfire validation, validation-based selection checked against independent outcomes, spatial ROC adjustment, spatial null surrogates, or wildfire risk-map-driven drone routing.

I agree with the earlier Codex review that a smoothed burn climatology is an established reference and that chronology needs explicit evidence. I would go further on two points. **Baker and Finn, alongside Milà, make P3 an application of a previously tested selection question**, rather than a new evaluation principle. And **“location-using models” is too broad even as a descriptive claim**, given the primary hybrid results. I used the earlier review for comparison and checked Moran’s final published PDF independently. [Earlier Codex review](/C:/Users/CORSAIR/projects/ThermalTwin/thermal-twin/repo/docs/scaleup/reviews/codex_20261116_round1.md)

**The single addition most likely to raise acceptance odds is one frozen, independently scored deployment test** comparing a predeclared, deployment-aligned selector and the focal-AUC selectors in new landscapes or seasons, with the history reference in the candidate set. Fix the issuance date, common observable domain and complete horizon before scoring. Measure regret on one primary proper score and report matched-area capture as the operational outcome. Include observed sites with no later burns in the proper-score analysis. The current 30-positive-cell rule makes the AUC result conditional on future burning support, which an operational user cannot know at issuance. With that test and reusable evaluation code, EMS has a model-evaluation contribution with relevance beyond this fire cohort. Without it, the plausible desk-reject reading is a careful case study that confirms earlier spatial-validation findings.
